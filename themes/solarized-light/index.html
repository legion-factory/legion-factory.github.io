Where the palette comes from

Solarized was designed by Ethan Schoonover and released in 2011 in the altercation/solarized repository under the MIT license. It is a sixteen-color palette — eight monotones and eight accent colors — built for both terminal and GUI applications.

Its author describes it as “precision colors for machines and people”: the colors were chosen with precise CIELAB lightness relationships and hues based on fixed color-wheel relationships, and were tested on calibrated and deliberately miscalibrated displays in different lighting conditions. The same accent colors are shared by the light and dark modes, so switching between them keeps contrast relationships intact.

Why people use it

Solarized is one of the most widely ported color schemes for developers: the original repository has more than 16,000 stars on GitHub, and its README lists ports for Vim, Emacs, IntelliJ IDEA, NetBeans, TextMate, Visual Studio, iTerm2, Terminal.app, PuTTY, Xresources and more. Many of these ports were contributed by other people and are credited by name in the project.

The project is presented mainly through its project page and GitHub README, which explain the design method, show the palette and screenshots in many applications, and invite others to contribute ports. Its popularity grew through that community rather than through advertising.
